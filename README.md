## 1. Class Structure

Our robotics and STEM coursework combines concept instruction, written practice, hands-on building, testing, and reflection. Current electrical engineering topics include resistor identification, color bands, resistance, LEDs, circuit diagrams, and measurements with a multimeter.

Students work in grade groups: [Grades 5–6](Grades_5-6/), [Grades 7–8](Grades_7-8/), and [Grades 9–10](Grades_9-10/). Assignments and the depth of analysis vary by grade group.

A typical class includes an opening review or bell ringer, instruction or a demonstration, independent or hands-on work, and a written record of calculations, observations, or conclusions. Follow the teacher’s directions for the assignment and due date.

Bring your dedicated robotics notebook to every class. Keep completed assignments, diagrams, reference projects, and lab records organized and up to date.

## 2. Robotics Notebook Requirement

Every student is required to have a dedicated robotics notebook and bring it to class each day.

- **Missing notebook:** A **15-percentage-point deduction from the student’s total score for that day**. For example, **100% becomes 85%**.
- **Loose-leaf paper:** Students may temporarily complete assignments on loose-leaf paper if they forget their notebook. However, all work must be transferred into their robotics notebook before the next graded notebook check.
- **Unrelated work:** Work unrelated to robotics or our STEM coursework in the notebook will result in a **5-percentage-point deduction from the notebook check grade**. For example, **100% becomes 95%**.

**Notebook checks are graded assignments.** Students should keep their work complete, organized, and up to date.

## 3. Required Resistor Reference Project

All students must have their completed resistor reference project from last quarter in their robotics notebook.

This project is essential to our current electrical engineering coursework. Students use their resistor color-band charts and diagrams to identify resistor values, interpret color bands, and complete calculations.

Students who have not completed this project should do so as soon as possible.

Accuracy, neatness, and organization are especially important. Students must be able to understand their own drawings and use them as reliable references during assignments.

## 6. How Grades Are Applied

Students are responsible for completing assigned work, showing the required calculations and reasoning, participating in class, and maintaining their robotics notebook. Follow the instructions and scoring criteria provided for each assignment, project, lab, or notebook check.

| Graded item or situation | How it applies |
| --- | --- |
| Written assignments and lab work | Show calculations, reasoning, and steps where applicable. Final answers alone are not sufficient. |
| Notebook checks | Graded assignments. Keep work complete, organized, and up to date; transfer temporary loose-leaf work before the next graded check. |
| Missing robotics notebook | Subtract **15 percentage points from the total score for that day**; for example, 100% → 85%. |
| Unrelated work in the robotics notebook | Subtract **5 percentage points from the notebook check grade**; for example, 100% → 95%. |
| Required resistor reference project | Keep the completed project from last quarter in the notebook. Accuracy, neatness, organization, and usable drawings are essential. |

The deductions above are **percentage points**, not a percentage of the earned score. Each deduction applies to the score identified above.

## 8. Hands-On Work Completed Outside School / Photo Evidence

I may accept hands-on work completed outside school hours. Acceptance of this work is subject to the teacher’s approval; students should confirm arrangements for incomplete work with me.

**Students must provide photo evidence showing themselves with every required hands-on build, interactive activity, and iteration or version.** The evidence must identify the student completing the work and clearly show the corresponding build or activity.

### What to Submit

- **Include the student in each photo**, together with the specific build, interactive portion, or iteration being documented. The student and their work must be clearly visible.
- **Photograph every required version.** If an activity asks for five builds or versions, submit at least five photos: one showing the student with each required version.
- **Cover every hands-on activity within the assignment.** If an assignment includes several activities, and each activity has its own iterations, photograph every required iteration of every activity.
- **Document each iteration before changing the build.** A photo of only the final design does not document earlier versions.
- **Label the evidence** with the student’s name, assignment, activity or challenge number, and iteration or version number so I can match each photo to the instructions.
- **Submit the written work as well.** Photos accompany the required calculations, measurements, observations, explanations, and notebook work.

**Example:** An assignment has two hands-on activities. Activity 1 requires three versions, and Activity 2 requires two versions. Submit **at least five photos total**, each showing the student with the corresponding version: Activity 1 / Versions 1, 2, and 3; Activity 2 / Versions 1 and 2.

### Where to Submit

Send photos directly to the teacher through the private submission method provided for the assignment. **Do not upload student photos to this public GitHub repository.** Ask the teacher for submission instructions if a private method has not been specified.

Students completing work outside school hours must arrange access to their own materials as explained in [Current Equipment / At-Home Supplies](#7-current-equipment--at-home-supplies). My personally purchased classroom equipment is not supplied for take-home use.
